## market:notchnook
NOTCHNOOK (Lo.cafe) — CONFIRMED FACTS

Price: $25 one-time (5 device activations) or $3/month (2 activations); 15-day refund; also bundled in Setapp; also `brew install --cask notchnook`. Current build 1.6.2 (26 Jun 2026). Requires macOS 14.6+. 59.8 MB. 29 languages (Crowdin). Homebrew analytics: 2,277 installs/365d — that is the direct-download tail only; Setapp shows 1,437 ratings at 89%, Product Hunt 4.5/5.

Feature surface (from the official changelog, Setapp listing and lo.cafe): an expandable "Nook" board of user-arranged widgets — Media Player (universal, any app, album art + audio spectrograph, swipe to change track), Calendar (per-calendar selection, all-day/multi-day exclusion, click to open Calendar.app), Shortcuts, Mirror (webcam, external sources), Notes, Timer, To-do — plus a Files Tray (drag in/out, Shift multi-select, Cmd+A, Cmd+C, Return-to-rename, spacebar Quick Look, screenshot capture, AirDrop out), a volume/brightness HUD replacement with a per-screen toggle, a Lock button, QuickPeek on track change, media + calendar Live Activities with fullscreen visibility control, notch appearance controls (height fine-tune, rounded buttons, custom GIF, hide-notch animation), a synthetic handler on notchless/external screens, multi-monitor support, and a built-in auto-updater. Roadmap/most-voted: Battery Live Activity (21, planned), Bluetooth Live Activity (17, planned), Prevent Window Focus Stealing (6, planned), "Pipelines" custom drop actions (announced 2024, still unshipped).

WHAT USERS PRAISE: the aesthetics and the Files Tray, repeatedly. "Should be built into the OS. perfect." MacStories singled out the tray + AirDrop as the standout. Its moat is track record and breadth, not depth.

WHAT USERS COMPLAIN ABOUT — and this is Isla's opening:
1. Battery/CPU. The single loudest, longest-running complaint. MacRumors thread 2463695 has the developer conceding on Discord "with reports of the issue not being fully solved for a bunch of people, we'll be investigating it further" and telling users to roll back to 1.4.6. One poster: "the dev's website has a useless background animation that pegs the CPU. Maybe they don't care about your battery."
2. Media doesn't auto-detect. Setapp reviewers report having to quit and relaunch NotchNook while media is already playing.
3. Permission nagging. "Constantly asks to allow permissions" despite being granted.
4. Focus stealing — an actual planned roadmap item, i.e. their panel activates.
5. External displays. MacStories called the software notch on an external monitor "visually jarring"; Now Playing draws over fullscreen content.
6. Broken widgets — Calendar failing to populate, Mirror not accepting external cameras.
7. Setapp build lag: "It's August 2026 and it's still stuck on version 1.5.5 from January."
8. No clipboard manager, no Tidal, no way to file a feature request.

THE STRATEGIC READ. Isla does not lose to NotchNook on quality — it loses on distribution and breadth. Four of NotchNook's eight top complaints are things Isla's architecture already answers by construction: the non-activating NSPanel cannot steal focus (their planned feature is Isla's invariant); NowPlayingFeed reads MediaRemote through the helper so there is no "relaunch to detect media"; Isla claims exactly one entitlement and prompts only when the AppleScript fallback first fires; Isla picks the notched screen rather than painting a fake notch on every monitor. Isla also already ships three things NotchNook does not have at any price: clipboard history, translation, and word-synced lyrics — plus a lock-screen card with audio-output switching that beats their "Lock button."

What Isla is missing splits cleanly into two piles. The cheap, high-return pile: AirDrop out of the Shelf (NSSharingService, ~an afternoon, and it is the single most-praised NotchNook feature), Quick Look on spacebar, rename in place, a battery Live Activity (public IOKit, and it is NotchNook's #1 unshipped request), swipe/scroll gestures on the compact island, and shipping in more than two languages. The expensive pile: Developer ID + notarization, an auto-updater, the volume/brightness HUD replacement, and multi-display presence.

THE ONE THING THAT ACTUALLY BLOCKS "WORTH PAYING FOR": Isla v0.1.0 is an unsigned DMG. NotchNook is notarized, on Setapp, on Homebrew, and self-updating. No amount of motion polish clears the worth-paying bar while Gatekeeper tells a first-time user the app is damaged. $99/yr Apple Developer Program + Sparkle + a Homebrew cask is the highest-leverage work on this list, and none of it is UI.

## market:alcove
ALCOVE (tryalcove.com, by Henrik Ruscon — also of Klack). Bundle id `com.henrikruscon.Alcove`. Launched Nov 2024 (#4 Product of the Day, Product Hunt, 2024-11-16); current shipping version 1.7.7 per the Homebrew cask, with the public changelog repo (github.com/henrikruscon/alcove-releases) tracking 46 releases from 1.0.1 to 1.7.2. PRICE: $14.99 one-time on the official site today (reviews from Feb 2025 quote $16.99, AlternativeTo says $17 — the price has moved DOWN, not up). 72-hour trial, 14-day no-questions refund, license good for 3 devices with in-app device management, all future updates included, no subscription, NOT on Setapp (verified — Setapp carries NotchNook/MacNotch/Notchie/Notchkin, not Alcove), NOT on the Mac App Store. Direct-download DMG from download.tryalcove.com, signed + notarized enough to ship via Homebrew cask with `auto_updates: true`. Requires macOS >= 15 per the cask (marketing pages saying "macOS 14 Sonoma" are stale).

WHAT IT ACTUALLY IS: Alcove is a *system-status* island, not a productivity island. Its 46-release changelog is dominated by four things Isla has none of: (1) custom volume/brightness HUDs that suppress and replace the native macOS HUD, (2) Bluetooth/AirPods connect reveals with 3D device models and battery, (3) a full Calendar surface — widget, live activity, expanded month grid, click-to-join Zoom, Fantastical + Notion Calendar support, time-to-leave notifications, and (4) Focus/Weather/Battery/Connectivity widgets. Plus a real multi-activity model: several live activities coexist, you cycle them with a swipe-down or middle-click, dismiss them, and restore dismissed ones. It has ZERO of Isla's differentiators: no file tray/shelf (promised publicly since the 1.3.0 notes of Sept 2025 and STILL unshipped as of 1.7.2 in April 2026 — this is Alcove's single most-cited gap), no clipboard history, no translation, no lyrics.

PRAISED: near-universally for feeling native. MacSources scored it 99% with an empty "The Bad" section and called it "a truly native looking Mac app"; Today on Mac (Mahdi Yusuf, ex-Apple) said it "looks and feels as though it were born on macOS"; competitor comparison sites concede "probably the most native-looking so far due to its design and animations" and "excellent animation and gesture feel." The changelog shows *why* — entries like "Reworked waveform to visually match iOS," "Decreased kerning in marquee text to better match iOS," "Darkened lock screen icon to better match iOS," "Reworked Alcove to fully match Tahoe style." That is the native bar, and Alcove clears it by obsessive iOS mimicry plus five straight releases (1.6.6–1.6.12) that are nothing but stutter/lag/race-condition fixes.

CRITICIZED — and these are Isla's openings: (a) SIX-MONTH ABANDONMENT. Releases stop dead at 1.2.5 on 2025-02-25 and do not resume until 1.2.6 on 2025-08-21 ("Thank you for your incredible patience"). During that window macOS 15.4 broke MediaRemote system-audio reads and Alcove showed no artwork/title/duration for anything except Spotify and Apple Music — exactly the failure Isla's perl+dylib helper was built to survive. A reviewer dropped it to 1 star over this. Single-developer bus factor is the loudest structural criticism. (b) SCOPE: "doesn't have many extra features beyond media controls, like Tray, Mirror, or Calendar" (the calendar has since shipped; the tray has not). (c) STABILITY DEBT: the changelog itself is the evidence — "Fixed loop causing high CPU usage," "Prevented focus manager causing 100% CPU usage," "Fixed album art dominant color edge case 100% CPU usage," "Fixed calendar filtering CPU hog," repeated lock-screen crashes, "Fixed pill animation crash," plus a 1.7.1 hotfix one day after 1.7.0 and a 1.7.2 the day after that. (d) PRIVATE-API FRAGILITY, admitted in their own FAQ: "relies on private APIs that have remained functional for years" with no guarantee. (e) Today on Mac flagged the price as steep and "occasional minor UI quirks, including blur issues on certain displays." (f) Alcove needs Accessibility permission for the HUD (reworked in 1.6.2 to depend on it), Calendar, Location (weather) and Bluetooth — four consent prompts, against Isla's one entitlement and zero launch-time prompts.

## market:mediamate-dynamicnotch
MediaMate and DynamicLake Pro are the two cheapest paid notch apps and they attack opposite ends of the problem. MediaMate (€6.99/~$8 one-time on Gumroad, $6.99 on MacUpdate, macOS 13+, free demo mode, closed source, Touch Bar Macs supported) is a HUD-replacement app: it replaces the system volume, display-brightness and keyboard-brightness HUDs with compact iOS-style ones, adds a Now Playing HUD that can render "inside" the notch or as a floating bubble, ships four themes (iOS / circular / notch / classic), shows AirPods/Beats output icons, supports keyboard-brightness shortcuts, Focus Filters that hide HUDs per Focus mode, click-and-drag interactive HUDs, and defers external-display brightness/volume to Lunar or BetterDisplay. MacStories — the only independent, non-vendor review I found — called it "stable and well-designed with smooth animations all around" and "one of those small Mac apps... that you forget is not even a part of the native macOS experience" ($9.20 at review time). Its only criticism is scope: no shelf, no clipboard, no notch hub. DynamicLake Pro ($13.99 one-time, 3 devices, 14-day refund, macOS 14.0+ through macOS 26/27, notch and non-notch, signed+notarized since v1.0.2 in June 2024, no trial) is the maximalist: DynaKeys (volume/brightness/keyboard backlight), DynaMusic + Slim Player + music queue, DynaGlance (calendar + weather, weather on the lock screen), DynaClip and DynaDrop (drag-drop, compress, Quick Look, move, AirDrop, shareable link, audio/video/document/RTF conversion), DynaConnect (Bluetooth connect/disconnect), DynaSwitcher (app switcher with live previews), push-notification mirroring for iMessage/WhatsApp/Telegram/Slack/Email, iPhone/FaceTime/Zoom/Meet/Teams call alerts, timers with full macOS Timer integration, battery alerts, Live Activities (music, Safari downloads), miniLake compact mode, and a Liquid Glass notch style. It ships every 5-10 days; the changelog carries "Massive CPU optimizations", a crash fix, repeated external-display work, and Greek/Turkish localizations — and contains zero entries for VoiceOver, Reduce Motion or Reduce Transparency across its entire history. Criticisms are consistent: it "depends on accessibility permissions and occasionally exhibits minor visual quirks after major macOS updates" (TheSweetBits), setup is "overwhelming with many customization options" and DynaDrop has a learning curve (MacSources), and "No trial, so you buy first and refund if it disappoints" (macnotch.io). ## market:boringnotch-open
Scope: the open-source notch apps (TheBoringNotch, NotchDrop, Peninsula, NotchNotes — plus Atoll, MewNotch and DynamicNotch, which are the OSS apps that actually matter more than Peninsula/NotchNotes on stars and momentum), what they ship, and where they fall short of the $17–$25 paid apps.

THE SHAPE OF THE OSS FIELD (GitHub API, 2026-09-03):
- TheBoringNotch (TheBoredTeam/boring.notch) — GPL-3.0, 10,581★, 970 forks, 349 open issues, pushed 2026-08-30. The category king. macOS 14+. Ships Sparkle auto-update, a Homebrew cask, an extensions system, .xcstrings localization, and ~35 distinct settings.
- Atoll (Ebullioscopic/Atoll) — GPL-3.0, 4,235★, 179 open issues, pushed 2026-09-02. The most feature-complete OSS clone of the paid apps: live activities, lock-screen widgets, system monitoring, clipboard, color picker, shortcut remapping.
- NotchDrop (Lakr233) — MIT, 2,092★, only 5 open issues, pushed 2026-05-18. 27 Swift files. Does one thing (tray + AirDrop) and is the ONLY one of these on the Mac App Store (id6529528324) — because it never touches MediaRemote.
- MewNotch — GPL-3.0, 549★. HUD-first (brightness/volume/keyboard backlight).
- DynamicNotch (jackson-storm) — GPL-3.0, 534★, pushed 2026-09-02. Physics-first: swipe-to-dismiss, floating capsule on non-notched displays, hotspot/VPN/Bluetooth alerts.

- NotchNotes (oil-oil) — 78★, NO LICENSE FILE, created 2026-05-18. Closest architectural sibling to Isla: pure SwiftPM, Scripts/package-app.sh, unit tests, GitHub Actions auto-release, universal binary. Markdown pad + reference-only file shelf + sleep guard.

WHERE ALL OF THEM FALL SHORT OF THE PAID APPS — five things, in order of how much they cost users:
1. NOT SIGNED, NOT NOTARIZED. boring.notch ("We don't have an Apple Developer account (yet)"), MewNotch, NotchNotes, Peninsula all ship ad-hoc/unsigned. This got materially worse: macOS Sequoia removed the Control-click Gatekeeper override, and 15.1 removed the "Open Anyway" button for some unsigned apps entirely. NotchNook and Alcove are Developer-ID-signed apps from named developers with changelogs and support channels. This — not features — is the clearest thing people are paying $17–25 for.
2. STABILITY ACROSS SLEEP/WAKE. boring.notch's most-discussed open issue (44 comments) is the notch vanishing and never reopening after long sleep.
3. BATTERY/CPU. Multiple open issues; the always-on audio visualizer is the named culprit.
4. FRAGILE MEDIA PLUMBING. Apple Music's AppleScript/ScriptingBridge is broken in macOS 26 (Apple DTS thread 801357, unresolved). boring.notch closed its macOS 26 Apple Music bug as "wontfix" and told users to switch source.
5. NO REAL NOTIFICATIONS. The paid apps do notification mirroring; the OSS ones that try (Peninsula, Canopy) tail the undocumented ~/Library/Group Containers/group.com.apple.usernoted/db2/db SQLite file and need Full Disk Access.

WHERE ISLA STANDS. Isla is already ahead of every one of these on craft — 53 test files, release gate scripts, Reduce Motion / Reduce Transparency / Increase Contrast honored live, 76 accessibility modifiers, a hardened glassSurface path, a MediaRemote helper that is strictly more durable than the ScriptingBridge route boring.notch is stuck on, plus three things the paid apps charge for (lock-screen card, word-synced lyrics with click-to-seek, on-device translate). THE SEVEN RECURRING FAILURE MODES, ranked by how often and how loudly they recur:

1. MEDIA DETECTION DIES ON AN OS POINT RELEASE. The single largest thread in the category (boring.notch #417, 58 comments, 54 reactions) is macOS 15.4 revoking MediaRemote from unentitled processes. Apple has never responded to the public-API request (feedback-assistant #637, still open). Then Tahoe 26 broke the fallback too: AppleScript/ScriptingBridge against Apple Music returns nil (Apple dev forums 801357; DTS confirmed "our engineering teams need to investigate"), and boring.notch #779 shows Apple Music non-library/autoplay tracks reporting nothing. THIS IS ISLA'S CENTRAL DURABILITY RISK AND ITS CENTRAL ADVANTAGE SIMULTANEOUSLY — it already uses the perl+dylib adapter technique that survived 15.4, and its AppleScript path is only a fallback. Every competitor that leads with AppleScript is one point release from dead.

2. IDLE CPU AND BATTERY. NotchNook: 10–15% background CPU, developer-acknowledged, unfixed across releases, workaround is "roll back to 1.4.6" (MacRumors thread). boring.notch #1260: a user's heap dump found 2,243 leaked NSTimers from a blink animation, ~748 fires/sec, 26–41% idle CPU on an M2 Air. The mechanism is always the same — an animation timer that outlives its view, in an app that runs from login to shutdown. Isla measures 0.3% peak idle (docs/performance/2026-08-18-cyclop-0.6.5-baseline.md) and its own 0.0% gate is UNCHECKED; it runs a PointerWatcher polling at 60 Hz warm / 8 Hz idle plus an always-on 2s geometry Timer. That is 100x better than the field but it is not zero, and 60 Hz pointer polling is exactly the shape of code that produces these threads.

3. SLEEP/WAKE AND MULTI-DISPLAY — THE CATEGORY'S UNSOLVED PROBLEM. boring.notch #336 (44 comments, open since 2024, closed and reopened) — the island vanishes after long sleep and does not come back; users report it does not even appear in Force Quit, one restarted their user session and uninstalled. #174: the island jumps to the external monitor on wake/reconnect/clamshell/KVM because the app keys off the main display. NotchDrop's maintainer publicly gave up: "我不打算做多显示器相关的需求和修复了" — I'm not doing multi-display requests or fixes any more. ISLA IS THE ONLY APP I FOUND THAT HAS ARCHITECTED AGAINST THIS: NotchGeometry.current() explicitly refuses NSScreen.main, prefers safeAreaInsets.top > 0, remembers each display's cutout by CGDirectDisplayID so a glitched reading can't move the island, and NotchController runs a 2s watchdog that re-applies the frame AND the content/hosted view sizes when they drift. That is a genuine, demonstrable, marketable advantage. Its gap: it observes screensDidSleep/screensDidWake but NOT NSWorkspace.willSleep/didWake, i.e. display sleep but not system sleep — which is the exact trigger in #336.

4. THE APP EATS CLICKS AND FIGHTS FULLSCREEN. NotchDrop #23: the notch's hit region blocks clicking Safari tabs underneath. boring.notch #663/#239/#814/#1359: the island stays visible over fullscreen video, fullscreen window controls persist, and the auto-hide menu bar gets stuck down after hovering the notch. Isla's activeRect click-through model is the right architecture and CompactHitAreaTests/HoverRectTests exist, but there is NO fullscreen detection anywhere in the source — only `.fullScreenAuxiliary` collection behavior, which makes the panel JOIN fullscreen spaces rather than hide from them.

5. GATEKEEPER, EXPIRED CERTS, AND "CONTAINS MALWARE". mew-notch #21 and #50: macOS literally moved the app to Trash saying it contains malware — once from an expired cert, once because the app reads Apple private framework files, and VirusTotal still flags the DMG. boring.notch has ~8 issues that are just "can't open it" plus a Ko-fi fundraiser for a Developer ID, and #1460 is an open user request for signed builds. Isla v0.1.0 SHIPPED UNSIGNED, and it loads a dylib into /usr/bin/perl — the exact pattern that trips heuristic flagging. Scripts/release.sh already has the Developer ID + notarize path written; nobody has run it.

6. NO WAY TO RECOVER, NO WAY TO UPDATE. When the island vanishes the universal fix in these threads is "click the menu bar icon → restart widget" or force-quit. Isla deliberately has no Dock icon, no menu bar item and no window — ⌥⌘I is the only route back. And there is no updater at all (no Sparkle, no appcast, zero matches). boring.notch users at least get a broken update prompt (#855, #395); Isla users who installed v0.1.0 will sit on a build that an OS update silently broke, forever.

7. DISK BLOAT AND DOUBLE-HUD. boring.notch #772: "This app created 60 GB cache in just 3 months!! loved this, but deleting rn" — artwork cache plus untracked shelf temp files; a second user reported 20+ GB after the claimed fix. mew-notch #4/#5/#11/#25: the app's HUD and the native macOS HUD both appear, and auto-brightness spuriously triggers it. Isla has a Clear Screenshots Folder control with a size readout and a Clear Lyrics Cache — better hygiene than the field, though the shelf has no equivalent.

WHAT MAKES PEOPLE KEEP ONE INSTALLED (the honest answer to "worth paying"): three things, and Isla has none of them. (a) HUD REPLACEMENT for volume/brightness/keyboard-backlight — this is the feature that makes the app useful hundreds of times a day whether or not you ever open the notch, and it is the one thing Alcove ($14.99) is bought for. (b) THE AIRDROP/FILE SHELF — the most-cited "I actually use this daily" feature on Product Hunt, and the reason people switched from paid NotchNook to free boring.notch. (c) BATTERY + CHARGING + ACCESSORY LIVE ACTIVITIES — an ambient reason for the island to exist when no music is playing; Isla's island is inert without media. The top-voted unbuilt feature in the whole category is NOTIFICATIONS in the notch (#592), which is DynamicLake Pro's ($13.99) main differentiator. ## market:category-truth
Seven features, researched against sources and then **verified by compiling and running probe binaries on this Mac (macOS 26.5.2, unsigned, unentitled)** — /tmp/probe.swift, probe2.swift, probe3.swift. Headline: five of the seven have a fully public, permission-free API and Isla ships none of them; the two that don't (Focus mode by name, Clock timer mirroring) are the two you should not build.

WHAT IS ACTUALLY LEGAL AND FREE (verified running, no TCC prompt, no entitlement):
• Battery/charging — IOKit.ps `IOPSNotificationCreateRunLoopSource` + `IOPSGetPowerSourceDescription`. Read `cap=24 state="AC Power" charging=1 ttf=152`. Zero risk.
• Camera in-use — CoreMediaIO `kCMIOHardwarePropertyDevices` + `kCMIODevicePropertyDeviceIsRunningSomewhere` ('gone') + `CMIOObjectAddPropertyListenerBlock`. Enumerated 6 devices incl. Continuity Camera, status=0, **no camera permission prompt**.
• Mic in-use *with app attribution* — CoreAudio process API `kAudioHardwarePropertyProcessObjectList` ('prs#') → `kAudioProcessPropertyBundleID` + `kAudioProcessPropertyIsRunningInput` ('piri'). Enumerated 33 processes with bundle IDs. This beats Apple's own orange dot, which won't tell you who without a click.
• Bluetooth/AirPods connect+disconnect — `IOBluetoothDevice.pairedDevices()` / `+registerForConnectNotifications:selector:` / `-registerForDisconnectNotification:selector:`. Public and NOT deprecated in the macOS 26 SDK; returned 4 paired devices unentitled.
• AirDrop receipt — no public API and no usable distributed notification (I dumped strings from /usr/libexec/sharingd; nothing shippable). But the quarantine xattr is decisive and I confirmed it live: an AirDropped file reads `0081;6a986675;sharingd;…` where a Safari download reads `0083;…;Safari;…`. FSEvents on ~/Downloads + `getxattr("com.apple.quarantine")` is a durable, permission-light route. Spotlight is NOT — `mdls -name kMDItemQuarantineAgentName` returned `(null)` on that same file.

WHAT IS GENUINELY PRIVATE:
• Brightness — `DisplayServicesGetBrightness`/`SetBrightness` via dlopen of DisplayServices.framework; keyboard backlight via CoreBrightness `KeyboardBrightnessClient` `-brightnessForKeyboard:`. Volume is fully public (Isla already has it).
• AirPods battery % — undocumented KVC on IOBluetoothDevice: `batteryPercentSingle/Left/Right/Case`, `isAppleDevice`. **I confirmed all six selectors still respond on macOS 26.5.2.**
• HUD suppression — there is no supported way. Everyone (boring.notch, volumeHUD 3.0, VolumeGlass, MediaMate) swallows the key with a `CGEvent.tapCreate(.cghidEventTap, …, eventsOfInterest: 1<<14)` on NX_SYSDEFINED, returns nil, and applies the change itself. Costs Accessibility TCC. I separately confirmed the `com.apple.OSDUIHelper` XPC service still accepts a connection from an unsigned binary on Tahoe — but that shows *Apple's* HUD, it can't suppress it.

TWO TRAPS — DO NOT BUILD THESE:
• **Focus mode.** The legal API (`INFocusStatusCenter`, macOS 12+, confirmed in the local SDK) needs the Communication Notifications entitlement `com.apple.developer.usernotifications.communication` — unreachable for an unsigned app — and returns only a **boolean** `isFocused`, with no change notification. Unentitled it answered `authStatus=0, isFocused=false`, i.e. it lies quietly. The named-mode workaround (`~/Library/DoNotDisturb/DB/Assertions.json`) **is now TCC-protected on macOS 26** — I got `Operation not permitted`. Named Focus now costs a Full Disk Access grant. That is an absurd ask for a notch app.
• **Clock timer mirroring.** `~/Library/Preferences/com.apple.mobiletimerd.plist` is already dead — it reads `MTTimerStorageMigratedToCoreData = true` on this machine. State moved to `~/Library/Group Containers/group.com.apple.mobiletimerd/local.sqlite` (readable without FDA; tables ZMTCDTIMER/ZMTCDALARM, ZFIRETIME is a keyed-archive bplist). Apple migrated this schema *one release ago*. Ship your own timer — NotchNook, MacNotch, DynamicLake, Atoll and Notchy all do.

ISLA'S POSITION: it has zero of the seven. It does have the public CoreAudio half of two of them already (`SystemVolume.swift`, `AudioOutputs.swift` reads `kAudioHardwarePropertyDevices` and `kAudioHardwarePropertyDefaultOutputDevice` but installs no listeners), so battery + camera/mic + AirPods-connect are each roughly a day of work with no new entitlement and no new permission. That is a lot of competitive ground for very little durability debt.

CAVEAT ON COMPETITOR DATA: every "best notch apps 2026" comparison I found (macnotch.io, notchy.dev, getdroppy.app, tryonenotch.com, getseam.app, notchbay.com) is published by a company selling a notch app. Treat the feature grids as directional marketing, not verified fact. Prices cited (NotchNook $25-30, Alcove $5-15, MediaMate ~$7-9, DynamicLake Pro ~$14) disagree across those same pages.

- AirDrop receipt (a file just arrived) — has: NotchNook, MacNotch, DynamicLake Pro, Boring Notch | isla: no | why: Isla already has a Shelf with drag-source, ShelfStore and QuickLookThumbnailing — an inbound AirDrop landing directly in that Shelf, animated at the notch, is the completed version of a feature Isla half-ships today. I dumped notification-shaped strings from /usr/libexec/sharingd and found nothing shippable — com.apple.sharingd.ConnectedChanged, com.apple.airdrop, com.apple.sharing.* are internal queue and subsystem names, not a public broadcast. DO NOT build on them. The route that works: FSEventStreamCreate (or DispatchSource.makeFileSystemObjectSource) on ~/Downloads, then getxattr(path, "com.apple.quarantine") and split on ';' — fields are flags;hex-epoch;agent-name;event-uuid. VERIFIED live on this Mac: an AirDropped photo reads '0081;6a986675;sharingd;6AAF4E51-…' while a Safari download on the same machine reads '0083;6a98af12;Safari;…' and a Telegram file reads '…;Telegram;…'. So agent=='sharingd' is a clean discriminator, and the hex epoch gives you the arrival time. Spotlight is NOT viable — mdls -name kMDItemQuarantineAgentName returned (null) on that exact file and mdfind on that attribute returned nothing. Cost: the Downloads-folder TCC prompt on macOS 15+, which a Shelf app can justify. Durability: medium-high; the xattr format has been stable for a decade, though 'sharingd' is an implementation detail. Reading the unified log (log stream --predicate on subsystem com.apple.sharingd) is the Jamf-documented forensic route but is far too heavy for a UI app.
